## Evidence protocol for the full research phase

Maintain a spreadsheet with one row per claim: claim ID, exact proposed wording, deck/slide, source title/URL, publication date, period measured, geography, population, denominator, unit/currency, source page or section, observed evidence, calculation, caveat, reviewer, and last checked date.

Classify each row as: verified repository fact; production verified; external reported fact; vendor claim; estimate; hypothesis; founder supplied pending validation; or unresolved. “Source found” is not sufficient if it measures the wrong population.

For market research, prefer original reports and official statistics, then credible specialist trade reporting. For product capability/access, prefer current official documentation and hands-on observation. Archive public evidence where permitted. Do not republish report charts or lengthy excerpts without the necessary rights; redraw factual charts with appropriate citation when suitable.

Pricing records must capture currency, territory, tax treatment, billing interval, promotional period, plan entitlement, minimum seats, and quote date. Use “not publicly verified” when a quote is unavailable. Record feature absence only when supported by an explicit statement or a sufficiently scoped test; otherwise mark “not verified.”

## Priority research gaps

- Art practitioner and fashion professional populations with comparable occupational definitions and deduplication.
- India-first versus global English-language reachable audiences and distribution economics.
- Current access/pricing across the closest competitors, including educational/free offers.
- ArtConnect, ArtRabbit, Artnet, relevant social/community substitutes, and other platforms named in interviews.
- Customer evidence for fragmentation, time lost, trusted feedback, willingness to contribute, and paid value.
- Independent evidence of competitor quality where available; vendor claims alone should not determine rankings.
- Centoire actual runtime availability, source health, AI outcomes, active cohorts, revenue, paid commitments, team, and fundraising needs.

Do not introduce a numerical TAM or a ranked competitor “winner” until these gaps are addressed to the level required by the intended slide claim.
